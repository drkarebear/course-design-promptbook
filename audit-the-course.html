Audit the Course

Close the loop. Bring back the course-specific alignment rubric you created from the COR and use it to examine the course you actually built.

The goal: find important COR learning that is under-taught, over-assessed, buried, repeated unnecessarily, or missing—and make the smallest high-value repairs before launch.

Where you are

  2. 7 Check the Build-Up

Why this step matters

The rubric you started with becomes your final design check.

At the beginning, the COR helped define the course. Now the same course-specific rubric can help you see whether the finished design actually teaches the important learning—not merely whether those topics appear somewhere.

  1. 1Audit

    Review each rubric domain independently using evidence from the whole course.

  2. 2Prioritize

    Separate consequential gaps from small imperfections and optional refinements.

  3. 3Repair

    Make a short list of changes that improve alignment without rebuilding the course.

What you’ll need

Audit the course you built—not an imaginary perfect course.

📄

Your COR

Keep the official curriculum available as the source of truth when something is unclear.

📋

Your course-specific rubric

Use the alignment rubric created from the COR rather than a generic course-quality checklist.

🧩

Your actual course design

Provide enough of the weekly map, modules, assignments, activities, explanations, and resources for the reviewer to see how students are taught.

Important: when auditing a designed course, count instructor-created teaching too. A short lecture, model, worked example, guided activity, feedback routine, or scaffold can be meaningful evidence even when it is not part of the assigned OER.

Keep the standard high

Presence is still not instruction.

A topic in a module title, reading, discussion, quiz, or assignment does not automatically mean students were taught the underlying skill. The audit should look for meaningful instructional support: explanation, modeling, practice, application, feedback, revision, or other appropriate teaching moves.

No overall score. Strong and weak areas should remain visible. Do not average them into a percentage or one course grade.

Prompt 1

Run the course-specific alignment audit

Use the rubric domain by domain and require concrete evidence from the course.

Audit

Copy-and-paste prompt

I want to audit this completed or nearly completed course design against its course-specific alignment rubric.

Course Outline of Record:
[Attach or paste the COR if it is not already available.]

Course-specific alignment rubric:
[Attach or paste the rubric.]

Course design/materials to review:
[Attach or paste the syllabus, weekly map, module outlines, assignments, activities, instructional materials, or other evidence needed to review the course.]

Review the course across its full span. Evaluate each rubric criterion independently.

For each criterion, report:

  • Rubric domain
  • Rating — Strong Alignment / Meaningful Alignment / Limited Alignment / Not a Significant Focus
  • Specific evidence — identify where and how the learning is actually taught or supported
  • What is working
  • What is thin, uneven, or missing, if anything
  • Confidence — High / Medium / Low based on how much course evidence I supplied

Use the rubric's course-specific definitions rather than generic expectations.

Do not treat the mere presence of a topic, reading, assignment, discussion, quiz, or project as evidence that the underlying skill is meaningfully taught.

Because this is a course audit, count instructor-created explanations, models, examples, practice, scaffolds, activities, assignments, and feedback structures when they are actually present in the supplied course materials.

If coverage is uneven across the semester, describe the unevenness instead of averaging it away.

One piece of evidence may support more than one rubric domain when appropriate.

Do not calculate an overall score, percentage, average, or composite rating.

If the supplied materials are not sufficient to judge a criterion, say Not enough evidence to rate confidently rather than guessing.

Prompt 2

Find the highest-value repairs

Do not let the audit turn into a giant wish list.

Prioritize

Copy-and-paste prompt

Using the course-specific alignment audit we just completed, identify the 3–5 highest-value changes I should consider before teaching this course.

Prioritize, in this order:

  1. Important COR/rubric learning that is missing or only lightly taught.
  2. Skills students are expected to perform at a high level before they receive enough instruction, practice, or feedback.
  3. Course elements that create substantial student or faculty workload without enough learning value.
  4. Unnecessary repetition that could be trimmed to create space for more important learning.
  5. Smaller clarity or sequencing improvements only after the issues above.

For each recommended change, give me:

  • Priority — High / Medium / Low
  • Rubric domain or course issue affected
  • What to change
  • Why it matters
  • Smallest useful repair
  • Likely workload effect — reduces / neutral / adds workload

Prefer repairing or trimming existing course elements before adding new assignments, readings, or activities.

Do not redesign parts of the course that already show Strong or Meaningful Alignment unless a change is necessary to solve a higher-priority problem.

End with Leave Alone: identify 3–5 parts of the course that are already doing their job well and do not need more revision right now.

Prompt 3

Create a final pre-launch check

Turn the audit into a short faculty action list—not another report.

Repair

Copy-and-paste prompt

Help me turn the audit and repair recommendations into a concise pre-launch faculty check.

Create only three sections:

  1. Repair Before Launch — only issues that are important enough to address before students enter the course.
  2. Watch During the Term — areas where the design is reasonable but I should gather evidence from actual teaching and student work.
  3. Leave Alone for Now — parts of the course that are sufficiently aligned and do not need more tinkering.

Keep the list short and concrete.

Do not create a generic teaching checklist. Base every item on the actual COR, rubric, audit evidence, or course design supplied in this conversation.

Do not claim the course is officially COR compliant, accessible, accredited, articulated, or otherwise certified. This is a faculty design review, not a compliance determination.

Quick quality check

A useful audit should make the course clearer—not heavier.

✓ Good signs

  • Every important rubric domain has identifiable instructional support somewhere in the course.
  • Ratings are supported with specific evidence rather than impressions.
  • Weak areas remain visible instead of disappearing inside an average.
  • The biggest repairs are tied to meaningful course learning.
  • Some parts of the course are explicitly left alone.
  • Repairs are small enough to be realistic before launch.
  • The audit helps you decide what not to add.

! Watch for

  • AI assigns an overall percentage or course grade.
  • A topic mention is mistaken for actual teaching.
  • Generic best practices override what this COR actually requires.
  • The reviewer assumes missing evidence means missing instruction without saying what it could not see.
  • Every imperfect domain triggers a new assignment or reading.
  • The audit becomes a compliance certification.
  • The recommended course is substantially heavier than the one you started with.

The core workflow is complete

Design backward. Learn forward. Then revise from evidence.

A course does not have to be perfect before the first day. The goal is a coherent, teachable design that honors the COR, prepares students for the work that matters, and leaves enough room for you to notice what actually happens when real students encounter it.

After you teach it: return to the rubric with evidence from student work, your own observations, and the parts of the course that felt too thin, too heavy, or especially effective. Course design is a cycle, not a one-time build.
