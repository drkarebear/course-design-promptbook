How the Promptbook works

Design backward. Learn forward.

Use AI to widen the possibilities, not to make the teaching decisions. The Promptbook moves from the approved course to the learning students need, then to the materials and activities that support it.

See the workflow Start with Step 1

The central idea

Faculty judgment stays in the driver’s seat.

  1. The CORsets the boundaries.
  2. AIgenerates possibilities.
  3. Youchoose what fits.
  4. AIhelps develop it.

The recurring AI pattern

Explore → Choose → Build → Check

Most of the prompts follow the same rhythm. That keeps AI from racing ahead and gives you a decision point before a short idea becomes a full course plan.

  1. 1

    Explore

    Ask for a few meaningfully different possibilities. Keep them short enough to compare.

  2. 2

    Choose

    Select the direction that fits your students, discipline, teaching context, and own judgment.

  3. 3

    Build

    Develop only the option you chose instead of asking AI to expand every possibility.

  4. 4

    Check

    Look again for alignment, missing bridges, unnecessary workload, and places where the design drifted.

The backward-design path

Give each decision a job.

The sequence matters. It keeps course structure, culminating work, pacing, readings, and activities from being designed independently of one another.

  1. 0
    Understand the Course

    Start from the COR: what is required, what is representative, and what is still yours to design.

  2. 1
    Find the Units

    Choose the 3–5 major learning arcs before building details.

  3. 2
    Design the Destinations

    Decide what students might produce or demonstrate at the end of each arc.

    Work Backward

Why the OER comes later

Build the learning before you assign the reading.

If the textbook or OER becomes the course structure, it is easy to teach what the resource happens to contain instead of what the COR and your students actually need.

The Promptbook first gives the learning a home. Then it asks what kind of resource would help students do that learning.

AvoidChapter 1 → Chapter 2 → Chapter 3 → invent assignments
PreferLearning need → resource with a job → purposeful practice

Use lenses where they matter

Not every course needs a separate version of every prompt.

Modality, accessibility, adult learning, cognitive load, and audience should shape the design where they change the learning experience—not create parallel workflows.

💻

Modality

Adapt interaction, presence, navigation, and activity structure for in-person, online, or hybrid teaching.

🧠

Adult learning

Make purpose visible, reduce unnecessary cognitive load, and connect new learning to meaningful use.

♿

Accessibility

Design access into directions, materials, activities, technology choices, and assessment from the beginning.

🏫

Dual enrollment

Use as an optional activity or support lens when relevant. Preserve college-level outcomes and rigor while increasing transparency.

Three useful guardrails

Keep AI in the right-sized role.

①

Do not ask for the whole course at once.

Large one-shot outputs hide decisions and create more material to untangle later.

②

Do not treat AI output as curriculum evidence.

The COR and applicable local requirements remain the source of truth.

③

Do not keep adding just because AI can.

When a course already works, leave it alone. Remove or repair before adding more.

Ready to use the method?

Start with the part of the course you actually need.

For a new prep, begin by understanding the COR, then move to Step 1. If your course is already partly built, jump to the relevant step.
