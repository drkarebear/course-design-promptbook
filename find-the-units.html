🧭 Step 1

Find the Units

Before you build assignments or map the semester, explore a few genuinely different ways the course could be organized.

The goal: identify 3–5 substantial learning arcs that give the course a coherent shape without simply copying the order of a textbook or OER.

Where you are

  1. 0 Understand the Course
  2. 1 Find the Units
  3. 2 Design the Destinations

Why this step matters

Do not let the table of contents design the course for you.

Textbooks and OER are organized for publication. A course should be organized around the learning students need to do. AI can help you see possibilities quickly, but you should choose the structure before asking it to build anything.

✨Explore

Generate three meaningfully different structures.

☝️Choose

Select the one that best fits the course you want to teach.

🛠️Build

Develop only the structure you actually chose.

Before you prompt

What you’ll need

  • Your Course Outline of Record (COR)
  • Your First-Time Faculty Course Guide or course-specific alignment rubric, if you made one
  • Your OER or textbook, if you already know what you will use
  • Any major scheduling constraints you already know

No OER yet? That is fine. You can find the learning arcs from the COR first and match readings later.

Prompt 1

Explore three possible course structures

This prompt intentionally stops before weekly planning, assignments, or activities.

Explore first

Copy-and-paste prompt

I am designing a college course using the attached Course Outline of Record and, when available, my course-specific alignment rubric and OER or textbook.

Do not build the course yet.

First, suggest 3 meaningfully different ways to organize this course into 3–5 substantial units or learning arcs.

For each option:

  • give the overall organizing idea in one sentence;
  • list the proposed units with brief descriptions;
  • explain what makes this structure meaningfully different from the other options;
  • identify important COR expectations the structure helps foreground; and
  • briefly note any COR expectation that would need to be intentionally woven across more than one unit.

Keep the three options concise enough that I can compare them easily.

Do not create weekly schedules, detailed assignments, activities, or lesson plans yet.

Do not simply reproduce the chapter order of the OER or textbook.

Do not recommend one option as the best choice. Stop after presenting the three options so I can choose.

☝️

Faculty decision point

Choose before you build

Pick the structure that best fits how you want students to experience the course. You can choose one as-is, revise one, or combine the strongest parts of two options.

Prompt 2

Stress-test the option you chose

Before expanding the structure, check whether it makes any important course requirement easy to lose.

Check the choice

Copy-and-paste prompt

I want to develop Option [number or name].

Before expanding it, check the selected structure against the COR and course-specific alignment rubric, if provided.

Identify only:

  • important COR expectations this structure could make easy to overlook;
  • learning that should probably be reinforced across multiple units rather than placed in only one; and
  • any unnecessary overlap between the proposed units.

Keep this check concise. Do not redesign the structure unless there is a significant alignment problem. If you recommend a change, explain why and keep the change as small as possible.

Prompt 3

Develop the chosen unit map

Now you can make the structure more useful—without jumping ahead to the weekly calendar.

Build the choice

Copy-and-paste prompt

Develop the selected structure into a clear 3–5 unit course map.

For each unit, provide:

  • a concise unit title;
  • the purpose of the unit;
  • the major learning students should develop;
  • the major COR or rubric areas the unit supports; and
  • what students should be increasingly able to understand, analyze, create, perform, or demonstrate by the end.

Also identify any important course expectation that should recur across several units.

Keep this at the unit-map level. Do not create a weekly schedule, detailed assignments, or learning activities yet.

Quick quality check

Does the structure actually work?

✓ Good signs

  • The units feel like meaningful learning arcs, not chapter groupings.
  • The sequence builds understanding or capability over time.
  • Important COR expectations have a clear home.
  • Some learning appropriately recurs across units.
  • You can explain why the course moves in this order.

! Watch for

  • The unit titles simply repeat textbook chapter names.
  • One unit becomes a catch-all for unrelated requirements.
  • A required COR area disappears from the structure.
  • The three AI options were basically the same structure with different labels.
  • AI jumped ahead and started designing assignments or weekly lessons.
