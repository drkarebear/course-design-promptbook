A useful rule

Do not ask AI for the whole course at once.

Choose the smallest prompt that matches the decision you need to make now. Explore briefly, choose, build, then check.

Find the Units

Choose the major learning arcs.

Generate a few genuinely different structures before committing to one.

Open full step
  • ExploreExplore three possible course structuresCompare 3 different ways to organize the course into 3–5 substantial units.
  • CheckStress-test the option you choseCatch COR expectations that could be easy to lose before expanding the structure.
  • BuildDevelop the chosen unit mapTurn the selected structure into a clearer 3–5 unit course map.
2

Design the Destinations

Decide what students might do at the end.

Choose the destination before building the road toward it.

Open full step
  • ExploreExplore four possible destinations for one unitGenerate brief, meaningfully different culminating task possibilities.
  • CheckStress-test the destination you choseCheck whether the selected destination actually fits the unit and COR.
  • BuildTurn the choice into a destination briefClarify purpose, evidence of learning, and boundaries without writing the whole assignment.
3

Work Backward

Make the hidden learning visible.

Identify what students need before they can succeed at the destination.

Open full step
  • UnpackUnpack what the destination actually requiresSurface the knowledge, skills, decisions, and conventions hidden inside the culminating work.
  • BuildBuild the learning pathwaySequence learning, modeling, practice, feedback, and application where useful.
  • CheckCheck for missing bridges and unnecessary stepsFind gaps without automatically adding more scaffolding.
4

Map the Semester

Give the learning a realistic home in the term.

Use the calendar as a constraint without letting it become the curriculum.

Open full step
  • ExploreExplore a few pacing possibilitiesCompare different pacing logics before filling individual weeks.
  • BuildBuild the weekly spineMap the primary learning focus and what each week builds toward.
  • CheckStress-test the semester mapFind overloaded weeks, rushed learning, bottlenecks, and unrealistic pacing.
5

Match the OER

Choose resources because they have a job.

Start from the learning need rather than the table of contents.

Open full step
  • Name needTurn the weekly map into resource needsIdentify what each week actually needs before assigning pages.
  • MatchMatch the OER to those needsChoose specific material and identify whether it teaches, models, contextualizes, supports practice, or serves as reference.
  • TrimTrim the plan and turn gaps into search briefsRemove weak matches first and describe genuine resource gaps without inventing sources.
6

Build the Learning

Design practice that does real instructional work.

Generate options, choose one, develop it, then adapt only where the teaching context changes the activity.

Open full step
  • ExploreGenerate a few activity possibilitiesGet several short, meaningfully different ways to support one real learning need.
  • BuildDevelop only the activity you choseClarify the sequence, instructor role, evidence of learning, feedback, and time.
  • AdaptAdapt the activity for your teaching modalityAdjust interaction, presence, navigation, and participation for the actual modality.
  • OptionalAdapt an activity for dual enrollmentIncrease transparency and support while preserving college-level outcomes and rigor.
7

Check the Build-Up

Ask what every meaningful task builds toward.

Find orphan tasks, missing bridges, and workload that is not earning its place.

Open full step
  • TraceTrace every important course element forwardIdentify what later learning each meaningful activity or assignment supports.
  • TraceTrace the culminating skills backwardCheck where major skills are learned, seen, practiced, and supported before high-stakes use.
  • RepairMake the smallest useful repairsPrefer remove, merge, move, or clarify before adding another task.
8

Audit the Course

Close the loop with the course-specific rubric.

Evaluate the course you actually built, then prioritize only the repairs that matter most.

Open full step
  • AuditRun the course-specific alignment auditRate each rubric domain independently using evidence from the actual course.
  • RepairFind the highest-value repairsPrioritize missed learning and under-supported skills before adding more material.
  • CheckCreate a final pre-launch checkSeparate repair-before-launch items from things to watch during the term or leave alone.
